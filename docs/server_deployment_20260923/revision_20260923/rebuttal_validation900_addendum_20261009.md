## Response supplement

We separate the trained model from its prediction rule by reporting three outcomes for every accepted round-70 CelebA checkpoint: raw argmax, the method's native output, and a common root-only calibration procedure. The common procedure fits thresholds separately for each model using the same rule and that model's clean-training-root predictions; it does not impose one numerical threshold pair on all models. GuardFed's native output already uses this procedure, whereas the eight baseline native outputs use argmax. Consequently, GuardFed's native and shared-calibration results are identical; the comparison changes when the baselines also receive calibration.

The analysis includes all 900 accepted records, covering nine methods, both distributions, five scenarios and ten shared seeds. For each method and seed, we first average the ten distribution–scenario cells, then report the mean and sample SD across seeds. Relative to raw predictions, GuardFed's calibration changes ACC by −0.3191±0.0590 percentage points, AEOD by −0.026475±0.005444 and ASPD by −0.039979±0.005514. These are paired differences for the same terminal models, not effects of retraining or isolated aggregation components.

The comparison does not support universal superiority. In the native view, GuardFed's cross-scenario mean ACC, AEOD and ASPD are better than those of 6/8, 8/8 and 7/8 baselines, respectively. Under common calibration, the corresponding counts are 7/8, 4/8 and 1/8. These counts describe the direction of mean differences; they are neither seed win rates nor significance claims. In particular, GuardFed's shared-calibration ACC remains 0.8445 percentage points below FLTrust, while its AEOD is slightly higher and its ASPD is lower. We therefore distinguish end-to-end native performance from the common-calibration control and do not attribute the entire native disparity advantage to aggregation.

All outcomes and negative comparisons are retained. Fixed nine- and six-seed sensitivity panels use the same subsets for every method and preserve the main accuracy/ASPD interpretation; they do not justify selecting the more favorable AEOD panel. These remain exposed validation data, with mixed CPU/GPU inference and training environments. Low disparity can also accompany poor or constant predictions. Neither this analysis nor its ranking establishes untouched-test performance, statistical superiority, or a causal contribution of an individual scoring term.

## Manuscript insertion candidate

**Separating aggregation from calibration.** We evaluate the same terminal models using raw argmax, each method's native prediction rule, and a common root-only calibration rule. Thresholds are fitted separately to each model's clean-training-root predictions, without evaluation-label fitting. Across ten shared seeds, each first averaged over both distributions and five scenarios, GuardFed's native accuracy is 88.4199±0.6225%, with AEOD 0.0097±0.0030 and ASPD 0.0610±0.0047. AEOD denotes the absolute TPR gap. Applying its native calibration to the fixed raw model reduces the two disparities while costing 0.3191±0.0590 accuracy percentage points. However, calibration also benefits competing methods: GuardFed has lower cross-scenario mean AEOD than four of eight baselines and lower ASPD than one under the common rule. Its accuracy exceeds seven baseline means but remains below FLTrust. Thus the native comparison evaluates the complete pipeline, and its fairness advantage cannot be interpreted as an isolated aggregation effect. These descriptive validation results retain all seeds and trade-offs; the mechanism study and frozen final evaluation remain separate evidence requirements.
